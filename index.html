Step 1. Your time

New to this tool? A short how-to video shows how to use each step.

Watch the how-to video (opens in a new tab)

When several due dates land in the same week, it is easy to feel buried. This tool adds up the hours you enter and shows which weeks look crowded, so you can plan before they arrive.

It also helps you see how much time your week really has for your education. First you list the hours that are already taken. The tool shows the hours that are left, and you decide how many of them can go to homework and studying.

Your typical week

Enter the hours that are already taken in a typical week. A rough number is fine. Leave a box blank if it does not apply. You can set different hours for particular weeks in the next step.

Not sure about your hours?

Keep a simple note for one typical week before you fill this in. Write down when you are in class, at work, caring for family, and traveling. Round to the nearest hour. Then come back and enter your numbers.

The 8 and the 2 are only starting guesses. Change them to fit your life.

    Step 2. Your tasks

    A task is anything with a due date: a paper, an exam to study for, a quiz, a lab, a shift swap you need to arrange. Add each one, then guess how many hours of your own work it needs outside of class. Do not count time in class. Use your syllabus for the due dates.

    The sizes are rough guesses. If you know how long something takes you, type the hours in the "Or type the hours" box. That box wins over the size.

    Weeks when your hours are different (optional)

    For a week with extra work shifts, a trip, or a day off, enter any date in that week and the hours you will have for homework and studying that week. For this week, enter the hours from today through Sunday.
